About

Studying political behavior and human capital through unstructured data.

I study how people and political actors respond to their social and institutional environments. My work combines experimental and quasi-experimental research designs with text, audio, video, and survey data.

I am a PhD candidate at the Munich Graduate School of Economics at LMU Munich and a Research Associate at the Chair of Data Science in Business and Economics at the University of Würzburg. I am also a Junior Researcher at C-BEAM.

Beyond economics: The first academic conference I ever attended was not in economics: it was a conference on newts and salamanders—the 2013 annual meeting of AG Urodela. The subject remains a personal passion, and I keep newts myself.

Facing the Far Right, or Looking Away? Video Evidence from Parliament

Legislators do not only cast votes: they allocate public attention, and how much of it a far-right party receives is decided speech by speech. Confronting its claims may give it publicity; withholding attention may leave them uncontested. I study how legislators from established parties respond to Germany's far-right AfD. To capture these responses, I build a new corpus of more than 23,000 parliamentary speeches combining words, images, and sound from the recordings. I measure engagement through whether a legislator names the party and how much of a speech they spend turned toward its members, a form of attention observable only in the recordings. Committee seats cannot be divided, so the AfD's share of a legislator's committee colleagues differs across committees of different sizes. Across 9,376 speeches by 269 legislators, increasing that share by 3.2 percentage points—from the lowest to the highest exposure observed—reduces engagement by 0.41 standard deviations. Legislators name the AfD less often and spend a smaller share of each speech turned toward its members. The decline in naming is concentrated in negatively worded sentences. These findings show how sustained interaction with far-right legislators changes the public attention individual politicians give that party.
