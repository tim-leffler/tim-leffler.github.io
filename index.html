About

Research at the intersection of behavior, politics, and unstructured data.

I study how people and political actors respond to their social and institutional environments. My work combines experimental and quasi-experimental research designs with text, audio, video, and survey data.

I am also a Research Associate at the Chair of Data Science in Business and Economics at the University of Würzburg and a Junior Researcher at C-BEAM.

Beyond economics: The first academic conference I ever attended was not in economics: it was a conference on newts and salamanders—the 2013 annual meeting of AG Urodela. The subject remains a personal passion, and I keep newts myself.

Facing the Far Right: Contact, Attention, and Elite Behavior in Parliament

Political elites' responses to outgroups shape how democratic institutions work, yet evidence on how contact changes behavior comes almost entirely from ordinary citizens. We study the entry of the far-right AfD into the German Bundestag and use an indivisibility in committee-seat allocation that varies how many AfD colleagues mainstream legislators work with. Using more than 23,000 plenary speeches with video, audio, and text aligned word by word, we measure whether legislators name the AfD and look toward its benches, how they gesture, and how they use their voice. Moving from the least- to the most-exposed committee portfolio lowers mainstream legislators' probability of naming the AfD by about 40 percent of its post-entry mean and reduces visible attention toward its benches. The decline is concentrated in negative mentions, and more-exposed legislators also take the floor less often. The mentions that remain are not delivered more calmly, and legislators still turn toward the AfD right after one of its members has spoken. This pattern points toward strategic avoidance rather than habituation. At the same time, the delivery of more-exposed legislators moves toward the AfD's vocal and gestural profile.

Digital Skills: Social Disparities and the Impact of Early Mentoring

with Fabian Kosse and Arna Woemmel

We study social disparities in digital skills, focusing on proficiency and confidence, and use a long-run randomized intervention to examine the effects of an enriched childhood social environment.
